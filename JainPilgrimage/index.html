THE WAYANAD CIRCUIT

Three places. Many lifetimes.

Begin in the south, where Wayanad’s granite tells its oldest stories. Continue north through living shrines and mountain air.

Showing 3 places
01 / 03HISTORIC BASADI
SULTHAN BATHERY13TH CENTURY

Bathery Jain Temple

A granite shrine of carved pillars and quiet proportion. Once a place of worship, later used as an ammunition store under Tipu Sultan — the name “Bathery” is said to echo that chapter.

Heritage monument↗
02 / 03LIVING SHRINE
PULIYARMALA · KALPETTA5 KM FROM KALPETTA

Ananthanatha Swami Temple

Dedicated to the 3rd Tirthankara, this hillock temple is framed by coffee plantations. Kerala Tourism notes its Dravidian details, carved gateway and stone Jain icons.

Active place of worship↗
03 / 03MIRROR TEMPLE
KOOTTAMUNDA · VELLARIMALAABOUT 20 KM FROM KALPETTA

Glass Temple

A distinctive shrine dedicated to Parshwanatha Swami. Mirrors lining the inner walls multiply the reflections of the icons and create its luminous, jewel-like character.

A NOTE ON THE RUINS

Some stories are
written in stone.

Near Punchavayal, the remains known in historical literature as Janardhanagudi and Vishnugudi are identified by researchers as Jain basadis. Their original idols are missing; the names and original purposes remain subjects of historical discussion.

Vishnugudi is at Puthangadi, roughly 500 m from Punchavayal junction. Janardhanagudi lies about 1 km from the junction on the road to Neeruvaram. These are fragile historic remains, not active temples.
